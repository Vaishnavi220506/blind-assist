## Why A stops

The frozen gate required>=64 recovered and target-contributor-recovered frames, FP<=61, side-pass duration<=1.4s, transitions<=93, no baseline alert/contact loss, and>=11 extra recoveries versus the head-frame control. Recovery and rotation-specific gates pass; both FP gates fail substantially. B is not launched.

Of103 added FP,100 occur on side-pass sequences. Their contributor signatures are:59 merged-only,23 merged+valid,1 merged+Radar,12 valid-only,4 Radar-only,1 valid+Radar. The other3 are approach valid, stop/back valid, and stop/back Radar. These signatures identify possible causes, not unique causal attribution when contributors coexist. In particular59 false frames trigger only from the inherited full0.02–4m merged-return uncertainty enclosure. Uncertainty preservation is appropriate, but treating any broad possible intersection as sufficient positive alert authority is too permissive. Do not silently tighten merged support, delete native contributors or claim it is the observed obstacle surface.

The result supports rotation-aware geometric reasoning, while rejecting this fixed union/possible-intersection alert recipe. It does not establish that the existing A* used the wrong coordinate frame, that a new classifier is needed, or that short memory would solve false alerts. No successor recipe is selected or executed here.
