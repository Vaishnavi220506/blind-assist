# MZ136: paired corridor ranking does not improve the joint alert tradeoff

**Keep MZ129.** Direct corridor supervision learns useful paired differences,
but this fixed lightweight recipe does not turn them into fewer false alerts at
retained recall and event timing. On the separate development groups both arms
rank all 24 aligned pairs correctly, yet their globally comparable positive and
negative scores overlap. Pair ranking supplies no measured alert advantage over
matched BCE. The result does not prove that cheap inputs lack learnable information.

## Actual change and matched comparison

MZ124 already did same-network BCE versus changed-cell hinge with complete
pair-disjoint splitting. MZ136 does not relabel that as new. The change here is
direct current-corridor classification: the original MZ120 network's eight
central near-cell outputs are latent scores, aggregated by log-mean-exp. Both
arms use frame BCE; only the candidate adds a signed corridor-score hinge,
weight .25 and margin 1. The 45-cell reconstruction loss is removed from both.
Cell outputs no longer carry a supervised localization claim.

Both arms have exactly 11,305 parameters, identical initial tensors, 2,400
batch-eight updates, four aligned pairs per batch, identical paired brightness/
contrast arrays, and AdamW lr .001 / weight decay .0001. There is one full
comparison seed. Full 640x360 RGB, original 8x8 two-return ToF encoding, Radar
and observed IMU preprocessing are retained; no pair/family/scene IDs, native
geometry or incumbent alert enters forward. Candidate scores are uncalibrated.
Each candidate produces its own alarm, with no incumbent OR fallback.

## Fit diagnosis and its disclosed change of criterion

The preliminary fit uses 24 aligned frame pairs from **four old scene pairs**,
48 consumed MZ123 frames. It is not 24 independent training scenes. The first
matched 800-step augmented fits reach 24/5/0 and 24/6/0 TP/FP/FN. Neither passes
the initially stated >=95% accuracy / >=90% both-correct at zero logit threshold.

One optimization diagnostic, 2,400 steps without augmentation, reaches 45/48
correct and 21/24 both-correct for each arm at zero threshold: the original gate
still **fails**. Each ranks 23/24 pairs correctly. A training-only scalar threshold
diagnosis reaches 46/48 correct and 22/24 both-correct for both arms. New capture
was admitted on that explicitly revised calibrated-fit criterion, before its
outcomes existed. These training thresholds were never transferred to new data.
The original failure is retained; the revision is not an independent validation.

This close-to-fitting pilot did not guarantee the larger source would fit. On
the new 192 training frames at zero logit threshold:

Each arm's 101-point sigmoid grid minimizes development FP while staying within
two recall percentage points of MZ129 and retaining its detected events with no
per-event delay over .25s. Development MZ129 is 24/13/0. Both candidates select
threshold **0.00**, i.e. the degenerate all-alert policy. Model/score/source hashes
and thresholds were sealed before the first held-out outcome evaluation.

The candidates add 12 FP and recover two TP; they do not meet the 20% nuisance
reduction target. Both rank 21/24 held-out pairs correctly but give zero pairs
with both final classifications correct. All-alert timing is not a useful gain.
No-alert/UNKNOWN counts are respectively 14/0/0; no-alert is never certified clear.

One fixed ToF sampling shift keeps 64 zones, nine rays per zone, signal budget
and reducer unchanged. Its analytic source is admitted against all 165,888 native
rays (max range error 8.36e-7m). RGB, Radar, IMU and all other public fields remain
exactly equal. Shifted MZ129 is 22/13/2 with the same five event detections and
.50s maximum delay; both frozen candidates remain 24/24/0. Since the candidate
readouts always alert, their unchanged alerts are **uninformative about learned
robustness**. The promised sensitivity check ran; no robustness success is claimed.

The old consumed 288-frame recheck is MZ129 139/93/5 versus both candidates
144/144/0. MZ129 detects 15/15 events with .25s maximum delay. All-alert candidates
reduce false-segment count 22 to 15 by joining errors into uninterrupted segments,
while false duration grows 23.25 to 36s. Fewer segments alone is misleading here.

## Why this is more specific than a failed threshold grid

After the primary held-out result, a **train/dev-only diagnostic** examined every
distinct score threshold interval, rather than the .01 probability grid. It did
not choose from held-out outcomes or apply a replacement threshold to them.

| Best diagnostic FP under the same retention rules | MZ129 | BCE | Paired |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Training |46|51|50|
| Development |13|23|23|

The coarse grid loses one additional development negative: it reports 24 rather
than the best exact-score 23 FP. Therefore it is wrong to say all-alert is the
only mathematically feasible threshold, but fixing grid resolution does not
produce the requested improvement. The exact development logit thresholds are
-4.691704 and -4.812388, and both still trigger on 23/24 negative frames.

At zero logit threshold both development arms give 18/0/6: five misses are
boundary intrusions and one is HEAD. Both rank **all 24 development pairs** in
the correct order. Pair-relative order therefore coexists with a poor common
decision threshold across scenes. This exposes three distinct limitations:
incomplete training fit; weak absolute scores on boundary/HEAD development
positives; and a coarse readout grid that exaggerates the already-bad tradeoff
by one FP. This experiment does not isolate representation versus optimization
as the underlying cause, and does not show that a larger dataset alone fixes it.
