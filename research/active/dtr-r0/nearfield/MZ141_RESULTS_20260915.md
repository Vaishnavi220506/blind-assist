## What changed

Same official527-key checkpoint, unchanged full RGB and regional-center sparse
input, same architecture. The239-key Depth Anything backbone is frozen.
Train5,420,458parameters exposed by get_lr_params: RGB/depth encoders, fusion,
prediction and refinement. Both arms use final-output metric L1; this deliberately
differs from the original paper's log-depth training loss, identically in both arms.
Only aggregation differs between the two new trainable arms. The paper confirms
that sparse reinsertion was intentionally removed; no reinsertion was added here.
[Official paper sections3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01596v2#S3.S2).

Pixel L1 pools known pixels across each4-frame effective batch. Surface L1 first
averages each visible object surface after removing the referenced8px ring, then
averages surfaces; this term and the ring mean receive equal mass. All other
known surfaces remain supervised, including env0 and rod shape1. Empty rings
do not create a zero loss term. Same valid pixels in both arms; UNKNOWN masked.

Scene0/1/2 in each family:144fit frames. Scene3:48whole-scene heldout frames.
Both arms use seed141, identical shuffles, Adam1e-4,8epochs,288updates, no
augmentation/scheduler/checkpoint selection or retry. A frame is the actual
microbatch;4gradient accumulations define an effective batch, so train-mode
BatchNorm uses single-frame spatial statistics in both arms. No loss sweep.

## Post-result normalization diagnosis, no weight updates

The final evaluation degradation also appears on fit scenes despite decreasing
training losses. Inspecting the official encoder found both BatchNorm and
DropPath: simply toggling model.train() would confound two changes. A bounded
diagnostic therefore uses the third lexical frame of scene0 in each family,
the SAME final weights, and all modules in eval except41BatchNorm modules.
Those modules use current-frame statistics with track_running_stats=False;
DropPath remains off in both conditions. No optimizer or running-buffer update.
Full checkpoint/state hashes remain unchanged; normal eval exactly reproduces
saved predictions on all8arm/frame comparisons. This is TRAIN-only diagnosis,
not an additional admitted model or heldout evaluation.

The mode effect explains part of the poor final inference on these four fit
examples. Gradient accumulation4 does not make BatchNorm observe four images:
it observed one frame at a time, and its running statistics were updated1152
times per arm. This is a limitation of the chosen training recipe, not a wrong
checkpoint load. Even current-frame BN leaves rod/shallow substantial errors;
neither sole-cause attribution nor a repaired geometry result is established.
The composite training loss is also not target MAE: the target receives1/4of
balanced weight in two-owner scenes and about1/6in three-owner rod scenes.
No normalization recalibration, new training or dev/test run follows.
